Project Description

June 2026-PresentIn development

3-DOF Robotic Arm

I am developing a compact robotic arm from the mechanical assembly through its ROS 2 representation and hardware plan. The system uses base yaw, shoulder pitch, and elbow pitch to position an actuated gripper for lightweight pick-and-place tasks.

Design targets

  • Reach and transfer a lightweight object with three controlled arm joints and an actuated gripper.
  • Size each actuator from calculated joint loads instead of applying one servo selection everywhere.
  • Keep the CAD, robot model, electronics architecture, and test plan consistent as the project moves toward fabrication.

Mechanical Design

I created the complete SolidWorks assembly and organized the base, waist, structural links, servo models, and geared gripper as separate subsystems. Mass properties from the PLA parts provide the moving masses and center-of-mass locations used in the engineering analysis.

I also used a conservative horizontal-load calculation with a 10 g payload and a 2.0 safety factor to size the shoulder and elbow servos. It produced design requirements of 7.65 kgf-cm at the shoulder and 3.88 kgf-cm at the elbow, making the shoulder the limiting joint.

ROS 2 Robot Model

I built a ROS 2 Jazzy development environment and a reusable robot-description package. The five-link URDF/Xacro chain defines three revolute joints, joint origins, axes, limits, and transforms. I integrated the SolidWorks base, waist, upper-arm, and forearm meshes, including a CAD-derived 120 mm shoulder-to-elbow spacing, and verified the model through RViz and the TF tree. I also wrote the Python launch configuration that starts the state publishers and RViz as one system. The recording shows the base, shoulder, and elbow joints responding to the joint-state controls.

Hardware and Validation Plan

The physical architecture uses an ESP32, a PCA9685 PWM driver, and a separate high-current 6 V servo rail with common grounding and fused distribution. The CAD, torque analysis, parts plan, and initial ROS 2 model are complete. The next phase is fabrication, joint calibration, coordinated motion, and repeated pick-and-place testing.
