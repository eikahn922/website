Temporary Motor-Stability Strap Support

An existing hip-exoskeleton motor support began separating from the surrounding structure, allowing visible unwanted motion. I rapidly designed a temporary strap interface that attached near the motor holder and wrapped around the wearer's leg to stabilize the assembly until a replacement pelvis became available.

Design goals

  • Create a fast, reversible support without treating the concept as a permanent structural repair.
  • Route straps around the leg without interfering with the motor, pelvis bar, or surrounding exoskeleton hardware.
  • Make attachment positions easy to change so several strap configurations could be compared quickly.

Repositionable Test Setup

I first isolated the motor-holder region in a focused assembly. The attachment geometry could move between positions, letting me compare strap routing and apparent support before placing the same concept into the full hip-exoskeleton assembly to check clearance and system fit.

Evaluation

The evaluation was intentionally rapid and subjective. I compared visible motor motion, apparent stability, strap accessibility, leg routing, and interference with nearby components. No instrumented displacement, load, fatigue, or safety measurements were collected.

Outcome

The selected CAD documented both the focused motor-support geometry and its full-system context. The design was not manufactured because the replacement pelvis was completed before fabrication, removing the need for this temporary workaround.
