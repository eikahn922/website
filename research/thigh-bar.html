Prismatic Thigh-Bar Alignment System

I redesigned the V2.5 hip-exoskeleton thigh interface to address alignment changes that occur after initial fitting. The mechanism adds a passive prismatic degree of freedom between the exoskeleton and the thigh cuff, allowing controlled vertical translation as the wearer moves.

Design requirements

  • Permit gait-driven translation without binding or adding an active actuator.
  • Reduce parasitic loads caused by hip-joint and device misalignment.
  • Keep the thigh interface secure, comfortable, lightweight, compact, and serviceable.

Why alignment changes during gait

Manual fitting can align the device at the start of a session, but soft-tissue motion, cuff migration, and the hip's moving instantaneous center of rotation change the relationship between the wearer and exoskeleton during movement. A rigid connection can turn that mismatch into unwanted interface forces.

Research to mechanical architecture

I reviewed alignment strategies including manual adjustment, compliant interfaces, and kinematic redundancy. Work by Näf and colleagues helped frame these approaches, while Chen and Zhou's guided slider architecture directly informed the decision to add passive linear motion at the thigh bar.

Final CAD response

The selected assembly integrates a lightweight linear rail with a wraparound thigh interface and preserves the assistive load path. The rail lets the motor-side structure translate relative to the cuff while constraining the remaining motion needed for support. This page documents a completed CAD release, not a physically validated result.
